**Why this paper is a strong fit for your journal:**
Our study addresses a critical gap in atmospheric science literature regarding data-sparse mountainous regions. While previous studies in Nepal have primarily focused on single pollutants (like PM2.5) or specific events (like COVID-19 lockdowns) within the Kathmandu Valley, our research presents a comprehensive, national-scale analysis of six trace gases and aerosols (NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃, HCHO, and UVAI). 

**Key Novelties and Contributions:**
1. **Methodological Innovation:** We applied Emerging Hotspot Analysis (EHSA) via a Space-Time Cube to characterize the temporal evolution of pollution clusters—a method previously unapplied to Nepal's air quality.
2. **Structural Break Detection:** Using BFAST, we statistically decoupled long-term trends from abrupt disturbances (e.g., pandemic interventions and extreme wildfire seasons).
3. **Advanced Climate Linkages:** We employed Wavelet Transform Coherence (WTC) and Granger causality to reveal scale-dependent, time-frequency relationships between atmospheric pollutants and ERA5 meteorological drivers.
4. **Physiographic Zonation:** Recognizing Nepal's extreme elevation gradient (60m to 8,848m), our analysis is distinctly stratified across five physiographic zones, yielding highly localized, policy-relevant insights.

We believe that our layered analytical framework and focus on population-weighted exposure align perfectly with the scope of *[Journal Name]*, particularly regarding environmental monitoring, public health, and climate change interactions.
