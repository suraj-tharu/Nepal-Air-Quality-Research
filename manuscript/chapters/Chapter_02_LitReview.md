# Chapter 2: Literature Review

## 2.1 Overview of Air Pollution in South Asia
South Asia, particularly the Indo-Gangetic Plain (IGP), is universally recognized as one of the most severely polluted regions globally. The rapid industrialization, unchecked urbanization, and extensive reliance on solid fuels for domestic heating and cooking have culminated in unprecedented emissions of atmospheric pollutants. The complex topography of the region, dominated by the Himalayan massif to the north, severely restricts the atmospheric dispersion of these emissions, leading to persistent seasonal smog and degraded air quality that threatens the health of hundreds of millions of people.

## 2.2 The Indo-Gangetic Plain and Transboundary Transport
Nepal’s atmospheric chemistry is inextricably linked to the emission dynamics of the IGP. During the winter and pre-monsoon seasons, prevailing synoptic winds facilitate the transboundary advection of particulate matter, nitrogen oxides (NOx), and sulfur dioxide (SO₂) across the porous southern border into the Terai and Siwalik regions of Nepal. The pooling effect created by the Himalayan barrier traps these pollutants, significantly elevating baseline concentrations independent of local domestic emissions. 

## 2.3 Satellite Remote Sensing of Trace Gases (Sentinel-5P TROPOMI)
The historical reliance on sparse and unevenly distributed ground-based monitoring networks has significantly limited the spatial understanding of air quality in complex mountainous terrain. The launch of the Sentinel-5 Precursor (S5P) satellite, equipped with the TROPOspheric Monitoring Instrument (TROPOMI), revolutionized atmospheric observation. TROPOMI offers an unprecedented spatial resolution of up to $5.5 \text{ km} \times 3.5 \text{ km}$, enabling the detection of localized emission hotspots, urban plumes, and valley-specific atmospheric dynamics. Numerous studies have successfully utilized TROPOMI NO₂, SO₂, and HCHO columns to track industrial emissions, validate bottom-up emission inventories, and monitor the atmospheric impacts of macroeconomic shocks, such as the COVID-19 pandemic.

## 2.4 Statistical and Machine Learning Approaches in Atmospheric Science
Traditional atmospheric modeling relies heavily on deterministic Chemical Transport Models (CTMs) which are computationally expensive and require highly accurate, localized emission inventories—data that is often unavailable or unreliable in developing nations. Consequently, data-driven Machine Learning (ML) techniques have gained immense traction. 

Algorithms such as Random Forest (RF) and Gradient Boosting Machines (XGBoost, LightGBM) excel at capturing the complex, non-linear interactions between topographic variables, meteorological drivers, and pollutant concentrations. Recent advancements in interpretable ML, particularly the SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) framework, have allowed researchers to move beyond "black-box" predictions. SHAP values quantitatively deconstruct model outputs, providing critical mechanistic insights into how specific variables (e.g., winter temperatures or specific wind vectors) drive localized pollution events.

## 2.5 Impact of Extreme Events on Air Quality
The baseline atmospheric chemistry of South Asia is frequently perturbed by extreme, episodic events. 
- **The COVID-19 Pandemic:** The global lockdowns of 2020 served as an unprecedented natural experiment, drastically reducing vehicular and industrial emissions. Satellite observations recorded unprecedented drops in tropospheric NO₂ across the IGP and Nepal, proving the overwhelming anthropogenic contribution to regional pollution.
- **Biomass Burning and Forest Fires:** The Himalayan foothills are highly susceptible to seasonal forest fires, exacerbated by pre-monsoon droughts. These events emit massive quantities of Carbon Monoxide (CO), Volatile Organic Compounds (VOCs), and light-absorbing aerosols (UVAI), posing severe acute health risks and accelerating glacier melt via the deposition of black carbon on snowpack.

## 2.6 Research Gaps in the Nepalese Context
While the IGP has been extensively studied, localized, high-resolution spatial analyses of Nepal's distinct physiographic zones remain scarce. Previous studies often aggregate Nepal into macro-regional South Asian analyses, obscuring the critical altitudinal gradients and valley-specific micro-climates. Furthermore, the integration of state-of-the-art interpretable Machine Learning (LightGBM + SHAP) with 8-year continuous Sentinel-5P observations to explicitly decouple meteorological drivers from topographic influences in the Himalayas represents a significant gap in the current literature. This study addresses these deficiencies by providing a highly granular, automated, and mathematically rigorous framework for understanding Nepal’s complex atmospheric dynamics.
