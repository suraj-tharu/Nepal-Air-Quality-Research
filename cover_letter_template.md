## Statement of Significance

Nepal's air quality crisis is disproportionately under-studied relative to its public health severity. Over 50% of Nepal's population resides in the Terai region — a pollution hotspot exposed to both intense domestic emissions and transboundary pollution transport from the highly industrialized Indo-Gangetic Plain. This study is the **first comprehensive, eight-year, multi-pollutant spatiotemporal analysis** covering Nepal's full physiographic gradient from the Terai plains to the High Himalaya using Sentinel-5P TROPOMI data and the Google Earth Engine cloud computing platform.

## Three Key Novel Contributions

1. **COVID-19 Rebound Quantified:** Using structural breakpoint detection (Binary Segmentation on STL trends), we precisely identified February 2021 as the month when post-pandemic economic recovery caused NO2 to rebound to levels 12–14% above pre-pandemic baselines in the Terai and Siwalik zones — filling a critical gap in the South Asian air quality rebound literature.

2. **Universal O3 Increase Detected:** A statistically significant country-wide increasing trend in background ozone (p < 0.001) was detected across all five physiographic zones — a finding with major public health and crop-loss implications that has not previously been reported for Nepal at this spatial resolution.

3. **Population Exposure Hotspot Identified:** By integrating WorldPop 2020 gridded population data with our satellite-derived concentrations, we demonstrate that the Terai region (>50% of Nepal's population) experiences annual NO2 concentrations exceeding the WHO 2021 guideline (10 µg/m³) by approximately 5-fold — providing a quantitative evidence base for urgently needed policy interventions.
